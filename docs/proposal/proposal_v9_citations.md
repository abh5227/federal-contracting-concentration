# Citation check for proposal version 9

Each row pairs a claim in `docs/proposal/proposal_v9.md` with the exact words of the source it cites. The quotes come from the saved copies in `data/external/` and from our own reports. Page numbers are the PDF page, with the printed page where it differs. The fit column says whether the proposal states the source exactly, paraphrases it closely, or rests on our own work.

Version 9 cites the same 18 sources as versions 4 to 8 and adds no new outside source. Citations [10] and [13] now link to the PDFs in the team repository. The 22 outside quotes are the ones checked word for word against their saved copies on 2026-10-07 for version 4. Every quote from our own reports was checked word for word against the repository copies on 2026-10-07.

Versions 6 to 9 call one agency, product code and fiscal year a segment. Our integrity report [10] calls the same unit a cell, so its quotes keep that word.

## Claims with no citation

These rest on our own computations or reasoning, so the proposal does not cite a source for them. Each traces to a file in the repository or to plain arithmetic.
- **Research on concentration has focused on defense.** This sums up the studies we found, [4] to [7]. Our search was not exhaustive, so the proposal says "we found no measure" for the rest of the government.
- **Each transaction carries a product or service code, a four-character label, such as 1510 for fixed-wing aircraft.** Check F01 in `config/flag_catalog.csv` tests that every product code has four characters. The label for 1510 appears in [10], in the table under "Large entries that were reversed later" ("Defense, 1510 fixed-wing aircraft, FY2020").
- **The measure counts prime contractors only.** The archive files are prime transaction files. `reports/archive_vs_custom_export_q1fy2025.md` matched the archive's FY2025 rows against USAspending's own download for Q1 FY2025, whose files are named `All_Contracts_PrimeTransactions`. Subcontracts sit in separate subaward files that we do not use.
- **The measure looks at one buyer at a time, so the antitrust cutoffs are only a rough guide.** This is our reasoning. The merger guidelines [17], [18] judge concentration across a market of all buyers. Our segments cover one agency's purchases, and a vendor that wins most of them can still face many rivals for other buyers.
- **DuckDB lets one laptop query all 88.7 million rows.** Stages 1 and 2 of the pipeline ran this way on one laptop.
- **The preliminary table and the 30% of single-vendor segments** come from `reports/proposal/concentration_prelim_by_year.csv` and `concentration_prelim_hhi_bands.csv`.
- **The $71 billion of the $130 billion rise, and the fixed-wing aircraft figures,** come from `reports/proposal/concentration_prelim_hhi_bands.csv` and `concentration_prelim_largest_cells.csv`.
- **Offers received coded consistently only from FY2012 to FY2017** comes from [10], "Some fields fill in or empty out over time". It says "FY2011 still has 64,905 competed actions with zero offers, against 14,557 or fewer in each year from FY2012, so FY2012 to FY2017 is the cleaner window."
- **HHI needs no inflation adjustment.** Inflation scales every vendor in a segment by the same factor. Every share stays the same, and so does the HHI.
- **The examples of 2,000 and 6,500** are arithmetic. Both agencies spend $100M on one product. Five vendors at $20M each hold 20% apiece, and 20² × 5 = 2,000. One vendor at $80M holds 80% and four at $5M hold 5% each, and 80² + 5² × 4 = 6,500. The same example appears in our team pitch.
- **The rising rule.** A line is rising if its average HHI over its last 3 years is at least 500 points above its average over its first 3 years, with runs at 250 and 1,000 points. This is our choice, explained in `docs/proposal/proposal_v9_methods_notes.md`, section 2. No source fixes the cutoff.
- **Sole-source share measured for umbrella contracts and for the orders under them.** This follows the plan in our team pitch, "Measure both ways, at the job level and the umbrella level, and say which is which." The fields and codes are in the methods notes, section 3, and still need checking against the FPDS data dictionary.
- **Features from each line's first 3 years and the starting vendor count.** This is our reasoning. A falling vendor count raises HHI by itself.
- **Competed awards with a single offer, for FY2012 to FY2017 alone.** The window comes from [10], quoted in the item on offers received.
- **Our total is within 2% of GAO's in every year GAO reports one.** [10] says GAO publishes exact figures for FY2022 to FY2025, "Our yearly totals match GAO's figures within 0.2% in the four years GAO publishes exact figures, FY2022 to FY2025."
- **The analysis methods** (k-means clustering, average silhouette width, L1-penalized logistic regression, a shallow decision tree, the adjusted Rand index and cross-validated AUC) are standard methods with no citation in this version. Each could take a textbook or original-paper reference once a saved copy can be checked word for word.
