# Government-documented gaps in federal contract data (version 3)

What GAO, inspectors general, Treasury and USAspending, GSA, OMB, the FAR Council and CRS have documented about gaps and errors in federal contract data, and what each gap means for HHI and top-4 share per awarding agency, product or service code and fiscal year, FY2008 to FY2025.

## Summary

Our project measures how concentrated federal contracting is, using HHI and the top-4 share within each awarding agency, product or service code and fiscal year. Those numbers can only be as good as the contract data behind them. So we searched what the government itself has written about gaps and errors in that data. The search found 280 findings in 202 sources, and 198 of those sources were read in the original.

Missing dollars turn out to be the smaller worry. Wherever the government tested them, obligation amounts and the awarding agency were among the most accurate fields in the data. The larger risks sit in the other two parts of our measure. Product codes changed meaning at two points in our window, so one code can name a different market before and after. The vendor field is the least checked field we rely on. No agency is responsible for parent IDs, and some records name a middleman or a placeholder as the vendor.

Some spending never enters the data by rule. Other transaction agreements (OTAs) run about $16 billion to $18 billion a year in recent years, and classified actions, most purchase card buys and buyers outside the FAR are absent too. These gaps define the scope of what our measure describes, and the final report should say so plainly.

Government testing of contract records is thin and uneven. Six of our 18 years had no test by GAO or an inspector general of contract records against contract files or agency records. Those years are FY2013 to FY2015 and FY2022 to FY2024. FY2013 to FY2015 also have no published total to check our data against, which makes them our least verified years.

Each finding has an ID such as E02. The appendix lists all 280 findings by topic, each with its source, page and period. `config/gov_findings.csv` also holds each finding's verbatim quote.

## What matters most for our measure

Five issues stand out. They are ordered by how directly each one can move HHI and the top-4 share.

### 1. Product codes change meaning at two points

The product or service code (PSC) is one of the three parts of every cell. GSA renumbered large parts of the code list twice during our window.

The first change took effect on October 1, 2011, the first day of FY2012. GSA renumbered the codes for facility-based services. 384 ended codes were replaced by 504 new ones, and combined codes were split. The old code Z111, for maintenance, repair or alteration of office buildings, became two codes, Z1AA for maintenance and Z2AA for repair or alteration (E03, E04).

The second change came on October 30, 2020, one month into FY2021. GSA ended 721 research and development codes and started 155 new ones aligned to budget functions. It replaced the IT codes with a new scheme on the same day (E01, E06). 139 of the 155 new R&D codes reuse an old code string with a new meaning. AC11 meant defense aircraft basic research until October 29, 2020. From the next day it means all Department of Defense military basic research (E02).

Old rows keep the code that was valid when they were reported, and FPDS does not convert them (E05). GSA also says FPDS cannot update a code on an existing contract to the current list (D01). Smaller revisions followed in 2015, 2019, 2020, 2022, 2024 and 2025 (E33, E34).

**What it does to the measure.** Cells keyed on these codes break at FY2012 and FY2021. A split leaves fewer vendors and dollars in each new cell, so HHI can rise by construction. A reused string such as AC11 suddenly covers a much wider market, so HHI can fall with no change in competition. The team needs to choose how to handle these breaks before building trends. The choices are to compare trends only within each code era, to roll the affected codes up to a level that stayed the same, or to build a crosswalk from GSA's code list.

### 2. The vendor field is the least checked

Our vendor is the parent UEI on each row. The government does not check that field. OMB guidance says agencies are not responsible for parent information, because it comes from the recipient's own registration in SAM (D04, D05).

The evidence on how often parent IDs are wrong is mixed, and the strongest-sounding figure is the weakest.

- **DoD, FY2017 Q2.** DoD's inspector general found the parent ID "inaccurate or unsupported" in all 57 sampled procurement transactions (D06). The same table also shows the country code, congressional district, address and type of transaction failing in all 57. The table combines inaccurate values with missing support, and its footnote says the parent ID is generated by FPDS. A failure that uniform points to a systemic problem in the first DATA Act quarter, or to missing paperwork. It is weak evidence that the parent values themselves were wrong.
- **FY2019 Q1.** Five inspectors general reported completeness error rates above 20% for the parent ID, and 13 reported none (D07).
- **FY2020 to FY2021.** Sixteen inspectors general reported parent ID errors and 22 reported parent name errors, which they traced to shared systems such as SAM and FPDS (D08, D09). At NASA, 62 of 242 tested transactions had a wrong parent name or ID. Officials explained that a parent change made in SAM does not flow to FPDS automatically (D10).
- **DoD, FY2020 Q4.** The parent ID had an accuracy error rate of 1.3%, against 11.0% for the parent name (D11). The ID is far more reliable than the name, which supports keying our vendors on the ID.

Our own parent test points the same way. In four corporate deals from 2013 to 2020, the parent ID switched 2 to 6 months after each deal closed. A lag of that size fits NASA's explanation that SAM updates reach FPDS late.

Two other problems put the wrong company in the vendor field.

- **Middlemen on consortium awards.** Many DoD and HHS other transaction agreements go through a consortium manager, which passes the money to member firms. FPDS records the manager as the vendor. One manager was credited with $7.2 billion in COVID-19 OTAs and passed nearly all of it to five drug companies (D19, D20, D21). Even after FPDS added consortium fields in 2022, 395 of 2,431 records in FY2023 and FY2024 still named the manager as the firm doing the work (D22). Most OTAs sit outside our data. HHS, however, filed COVID-19 OTAs as contracts and overstated its contracts by $1.55 billion (D18), so some of these rows are probably in our FY2020 and FY2021 cells.
- **Placeholder vendors.** The rules allow generic vendor IDs. DoD's list includes MISCELLANEOUS FOREIGN AWARDEES and an ID for consolidated purchase card reports (D14, D15, D16). A consolidated report can sum many actions in one row (D17).

**What it does to the measure.** Parent errors can split one firm into several vendors or merge separate firms, so HHI can move in either direction. Middlemen and placeholders pool many firms under one ID, which inflates HHI and the top-4 share in the cells they touch.

### 3. Some spending never enters the data

The FAR defines which contract actions go into FPDS, and several kinds of spending fall outside it by rule.

- **Other transaction agreements.** OTAs are not FAR contracts, so they are not contract actions (A14). GAO reports them separately from contract spending, at $16.5 billion in FY2020, nearly $16 billion in FY2023, $18.4 billion in FY2024 and $18.2 billion in FY2025 (A01). DoD's OTA spending grew from $1.8 billion in FY2016 to over $18 billion in FY2024. DoD obligated $62.9 billion on OTAs in FY2021 to FY2024, and 10 vendors received about 57% of it (A02). DoD reports prototype and production OTAs in a separate FPDS module and research OTAs not at all (A04). GAO found over $40 billion in OTAs from FY2020 to FY2022 that never reached USAspending (A03).
- **Classified actions.** The FAR bars reporting any action whose data would be classified, or that would compromise national security (A06, A07, A08). No size is published.
- **Small purchases.** Agencies must report actions above the micro-purchase threshold (A09). Below it, reporting is optional and needs written approval (A27). Purchase card buys below the threshold are barred unless an agency's own procedures call for them, and DoD tells its offices not to report them (A11, A12). Purchase card spending was $19.5 billion in FY2011 (F16).
- **Other channels.** GSA stock orders, AbilityOne store purchases, resale, concessions, training and interagency agreements are excluded. Actions paid with non-appropriated funds are left out unless the FPDS office approves (A13, A18 to A22).
- **Buyers outside FPDS.** FPDS holds no data from the legislative or judicial branches, the CIA, the NSA or certain DoD components (B04). In FY2012, the FDIC, NCUA and TVA also reported no contracts, citing non-appropriated funds (B03).

Two documented gaps sound larger than they are for our data. In FY2022, 49 of 152 agencies reported nothing to USAspending. The 25 executive branch agencies among them held just over $5 billion of $6 trillion in net outlays, under 0.1% (B05). Contract rows also reach USAspending straight from FPDS, so an agency that skips its account files can still appear in our data. Separately, GAO said in 2009 that it could not rely on FPDS alone for contracting in Iraq and Afghanistan, because not all actions were entered (B07). That gap could touch our earliest years.

**What it does to the measure.** These gaps set the scope. Our HHI describes reported, unclassified FAR contracts above the micro-purchase threshold. For most cells the missing dollars are small. Two areas are exceptions. DoD research and prototype markets lose a growing share of their spending to OTAs, which grew about tenfold from FY2016 to FY2024, and that OTA money is itself concentrated. Classified buying is missing at an unknown size.

### 4. The awarding agency can move without any change in buying

Our cells use the awarding agency, the office that signs the contract. Some agencies buy for others, and the dollars land in the cells of the agency that signed.

- **Buying through GSA.** In FY2024, $78.2 billion went through GSA contract vehicles, and GSA itself awarded $16.3 billion on behalf of other agencies (G02). Executive Order 14240 of March 20, 2025 directs agencies to have GSA buy common goods and services, about $490 billion a year (G01). GSA set up an office to take over contracts from other agencies (G03).
- **Shared service providers.** Some small agencies buy through a provider, so their contracts appear under the provider's agency code (B01, B02).
- **Reorganizations.** USAspending stopped grouping Army Corps of Engineers civil works under DoD on October 21, 2019 (E13). Background investigations moved from OPM to DoD by September 30, 2019 (E15). USAID's work began moving to State in 2025 (E14).

**What it does to the measure.** Agency cells shrink or grow with no change in what the government needs. Trends break at FY2020 for background investigations and at FY2025 for buying moved to GSA. The funding agency, which records who paid, can test this from FY2011 on. Earlier years carry a placeholder funding code.

### 5. Data quality differs by year, and fewer people check it now

The government says quality has improved over time. USAspending warns that more recent fiscal years tend to have higher quality data (E08). GAO faulted Treasury for not telling users that quality varies across FY2008 to FY2021 (E07). GAO's strictest test asks whether every tested field on a record matches the agency's own records. Only 2% to 7% of awards passed in FY2012 (D25), 0% to 1% in FY2017 Q2 (D26) and 24% to 34% in FY2018 Q4 (D27). One wrong field fails the whole record, so these rates are much lower than the accuracy of any single field.

Oversight has thinned since FY2021. The law requiring inspectors general to test agency data expired in 2021, and their last reports came out in November 2021 (F09, F10). The last governmentwide FPDS quality summary covers FY2019 (F07, F08). For FY2023, none of the 24 CFO Act agencies' quality reports met all of OMB's rules, and 19 of the 24 were late (F05). Only 36 of the 70 agencies that reported to FPDS confirmed completing a quality report. The other 34 held almost $2 billion, about 0.26% of contract obligations (F06). GSA paused modernization of its award systems in September 2023, facing a funding gap of nearly $100 million that year, and had put plans for FPDS on hold by May 2025 (F04, G17).

**What it does to the measure.** A rising or falling HHI trend can partly reflect better coding in later years. Error rates for FY2022 to FY2025 are simply unknown.

### What matters less

Several documented problems turn out to be minor for our measure, or already handled.

- **Late reporting.** DoD and Army Corps of Engineers data are held for 90 days before release (C06, C09). Our release came 11 months after FY2025 closed, so the hold cuts nothing.
- **Changes after the fact.** FPDS has no cut-off for corrections, and agencies can amend old contracts (C01, C03). Our results therefore belong to the 2026-09-06 release, which the pipeline records.
- **Competition fields.** GAO's samples found 18% to 54% of competition codes wrong (D60 to D65). That leaves HHI untouched, but any analysis that pairs concentration with competition inherits the error.
- **Terminations.** Several thousand USAID terminations were never entered in FPDS, so USAID's FY2025 totals are too high (G04). FPDS also cannot tell a complete termination from a partial one (G11, G12). Terminations also feed the negative vendor totals that the integrity report counts.

## Checks we could run in our data

None of these has been run. Each names the columns it would use. The first five matter most, because they test the issues that can move HHI.

1. **Product code breaks.** Count distinct `product_or_service_code` values by fiscal year. List the codes that start or end in FY2012 and FY2021. For each reused R&D code string, compare dollars and vendor counts in FY2020 and FY2022.
2. **Consortium managers as vendors.** Sum DoD and HHS cell dollars for the consortium management organizations GAO names, by `recipient_name` and `recipient_uei`.
3. **Generic vendor IDs.** Sum rows and dollars for the generic IDs on DoD's list (`LN9PU5M2YZN5`, `R5NWZ87HPLX4` and `U9X7H1UVY6U5`) by year and cell, from `recipient_uei` and `recipient_parent_uei`.
4. **Parent ID stability.** For each `recipient_uei`, count distinct `recipient_parent_uei` values within each fiscal year, and list vendors whose parent changes mid-year.
5. **Buying for other agencies.** Compare `awarding_agency_code` with `funding_agency_code` by year to size assisted buying. Track GSA's share of awarding dollars in FY2024 and FY2025, USAID (072) and State (019) in FY2024 and FY2025, and OPM (024) investigation codes in FY2019 to FY2021.
6. **OTA rows.** Search `transaction_description` and `award_description` for other transaction wording, and list HHS rows in FY2020 and FY2021 that match GAO's COVID-19 OTA awards by `recipient_name` and dollars.
7. **Vendor IDs across April 2022.** For awards with actions in both FY2021 and FY2023, count those whose `recipient_uei` changed between the two years.
8. **Consolidated reports.** Compare the sum of `number_of_actions` with the row count by year and agency.
9. **Small rows and rule changes.** Count rows at or below the micro-purchase threshold by agency and month around April 2008, January 2011, September 2011 and December 2012, and for FY2014 to FY2015 and FY2018 to FY2019.
10. **Terminations.** Sum `federal_action_obligation` on rows with `action_type_code` E, F, N or X by agency and fiscal year, FY2024 and FY2025, and count the negative vendor totals they explain. Check USAID FY2025 awards for missing termination rows.
11. **Agencies present.** List the awarding agency codes present each year and compare them with USAspending's list of non-reporting entities and the agencies GAO named.
12. **DoD's 90-day hold.** Compare FY2025 DoD rows by `action_date` month with FY2024 to confirm the hold did not cut late FY2025.
13. **A later release.** Recompute Stage 1 totals on a later Award Data Archive release and compare cell totals by year, to size revisions and any effect of the SAM.gov switch.
14. **Thin cells.** Count cells with fewer than five vendors and their share of dollars, to judge how many cells a 1% product code error could change.
15. **COVID-19 sensitivity.** Recompute HHI for FY2020 to FY2022 with and without rows carrying the COVID-19 national interest action code, and again with the COVID-19 disaster fund codes, and report the range.

## What we could not confirm

Each item was searched for in sources we could open. None is used as a finding.

- **Inspectors general found 16 of 41 agencies complete and 11 of 41 accurate for FY2017 Q2.** No government source we opened gives these counts. GAO-18-546 counts 53 inspector general reports and says 15 of 53 found data generally complete, timely, accurate or of quality. The 41 appears to be POGO's own tally.
- **A statute or FAR clause behind DoD's 90-day hold.** The only written authority found is DFARS PGI 204.604(2), which is guidance and first appears in its 2016 revision, plus an undated FPDS page.
- **Whether OTA records from FPDS's separate module appear in File D1 and the Award Data Archive.** No GAO, Treasury or GSA source says.
- **Which agencies make up GAO's $40 billion of unreported OTAs, and which of the 49 non-reporting agencies report contracts to FPDS.** GAO-24-106214 names none of them.
- **Any agency change in micro-purchase reporting practice in FY2015 or FY2019.** No OMB, GSA, DoD or CRS document records one.
- **Whether the purchase card data GSA gives FPDS become contract rows.** The FAR says the data serve reports only.
- **A GAO or inspector general statistical test of the product code field.** None of the reports we read tested it.
- **Why DoD's links between awards and accounts fell in FY2024 and FY2025.** No Treasury, USAspending or DoD note explains it.
- **That the fall 2025 lapse in appropriations delayed FPDS, SAM.gov or USAspending updates.** No government source says so.
- **How FPDS treats an ended product code on a modification to an older award.** No document found says.
- **Which awarding agency code FPDS uses for USAID awards moved to State, and for OPM investigation contracts moved to DoD.** No government source ties either move to agency codes.
- **Error rates for set-aside and size fields from a sample of prime contract files.** No such GAO sample covers FY2008 to FY2025.
- **Other items.** Version 1 lists 13 more, mostly sources that were blocked or sat outside the allowed sites.
