## Part 1. Data decisions

These decide which records enter the concentration score and how. The EDA ran them as variant V1, the decisions as Andy proposed them. Under V1, 3,241 of 357,284 segments (0.9%) change concentration band against the data as recorded, and the yearly share moves only in FY2008 to FY2010 (`band_changes.csv`, `concentration_by_year.csv`).

| # | The choice, in plain terms | Options | Evidence | Recommendation |
|---|---|---|---|---|

One, a $13.9B entry for Emerson Construction, crosses from FY2008 into FY2009 and makes one firm look dominant in a market of 1,039 firms. | Keep both rows. Exclude both. Exclude both and keep their small net as one documented adjustment. | Within one fiscal year a pair barely moves HHI. Across years it breaks two segments. ## Part 2. Project decisions

These come from the presentation, the peer reviews and the reframing note (`docs/eda/reframing_note.md`).

| # | The choice | Evidence | Recommendation |
|---|---|---|---|
| P1 | **Scope.** The civilian agencies as the focus, with Defense as the comparison, or every agency in the main analysis. | Defense buys from a handful of prime contractors and studies its own market. The class deck already uses the civilian focus, and reviewers accepted it. | Civilian focus, Defense as the comparison. |
| P2 | **The headline claim.** How to describe the trend. | The civilian share rose from 44.1% to 50.5% and passes a shuffle test (one-sided p below 0.005). Without Veterans Affairs it is flat, 45.4% to 41.7% (p above 0.35). Between FY2012 to FY2014 and FY2023 to FY2025 the share rose 7.2 points: Veterans Affairs added 11.6 points, and the other agencies together subtracted about 4. Twelve segments account for half of all upward movement (`trend_yearly_tests.csv`, `trend_decomposition_*.csv`). | Say that civilian concentration rose and that the rise comes from a few markets, led by VA health care, with no broad rise elsewhere. This answers the reviewers' "soften growing until tested". |
| P3 | **The rising rule for trend lines**, needed before any feature is compared with rising lines (EDA plan, ground rule 2). | At a 500-point gain, 54% of the 6,492 civilian lines rise, and 34% still rise with their years shuffled, so about 62% of those flags are chance. Testing each line against shuffles of its own years, with a Benjamini-Hochberg correction at q = 0.10, keeps 1,414 lines (22%) with $689B (`trend_lines_chance.csv`, `trend_lines_tested.csv`). | A line is rising when its gain is at least 500 points and it passes the per-line shuffle test at q = 0.10. |
| P4 | **What "competed" means on an order.** The column guide says the competition field on an order describes its umbrella contract, and marks it unconfirmed. | Concentrated civilian money is competed as often as the rest, so the revised story leans on this field. | One person checks the FPDS data dictionary before the story relies on it. Name the person and a date. |
| P5 | **Questions 3 and 4.** Reword question 3 around consolidation, and add question 4 on whether competition stays real at recompete. | Sole-source share differs little between concentrated and other civilian dollars, and the main concentrated markets were competed. | Adopt the revised wording and the candidate features in the reframing note. |
| P6 | **The second layer of money.** For VA health care and construction, the prime contractor passes much of the money on. | Subaward data covers first-tier subcontracts of $30,000 and more, self-reported, and probably misses payments to network providers. | Add a subaward check for a few flagged markets, and name who pulls the data. |
